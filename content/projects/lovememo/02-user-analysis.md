# LoveMemo｜User Analysis

> Understanding how couples preserve shared life — and why meaningful recording often fails to become a habit.

## 01. Research Context

LoveMemo was explored with **8 early participants** over approximately **one week**.

The purpose of this research was not to prove product–market fit or produce statistically representative findings. It was to observe how people understood the product, where interaction felt natural or burdensome, and which parts of the concept showed enough value to guide the next iteration.

The findings in this document are therefore treated as:

> **Early product signals, not market validation.**

They help refine the product hypothesis, but they do not establish long-term retention, broad user demand or relationship outcomes.

---

## 02. Users in Context

The project focuses on people in an ongoing intimate relationship who already create shared digital traces through everyday tools.

Their relationship content may include:

- Messages and small expressions of care
- Photos and moments experienced together
- Places visited or recommended
- Things they want to do in the future
- Everyday promises, plans and shared decisions

These users do not necessarily lack tools. The problem is that each tool organizes information around a different object — a message, image, task, event or location — rather than around the relationship.

For the early research stage, the project avoided turning this audience into a fictional demographic persona. The more useful unit of analysis was the **relationship behavior**: how two people create, preserve and revisit shared meaning.

---

## 03. Behavioral Tensions

### People value memories, but do not always want to “manage” them

Users may care deeply about shared moments while still avoiding deliberate documentation. Recording requires attention at the exact moment when they may prefer to remain present.

This creates a tension:

**Emotional value is high, but willingness to perform repeated recording work is limited.**

### Shared content exists, but lacks a shared home

Photos, chats, plans and places accumulate naturally. However, they remain scattered across tools and personal accounts. Finding something later often depends on remembering where it was stored.

The need is not simply more storage. It is a coherent context in which the content represents **“our life together.”**

### Structure can help, but too much structure feels like a task

Prompts, categories and check-ins can lower the difficulty of starting. When overused, the same mechanisms create pressure and make relationship interaction feel performative.

The product must therefore balance:

- Enough structure to make contribution easy
- Enough freedom to preserve emotional authenticity
- Low enough effort to support repeated use

### Individual motivation is not enough for a two-person product

The value of a relationship space depends on participation from both people. If one person contributes while the other remains absent, the experience can become emotionally uneven.

This means LoveMemo must be evaluated not only through individual usability, but also through **participation balance, reciprocity and shared ownership**.

---

## 04. Early User Signals

### Signal 01 — Recording can quickly become task-like

Early feedback indicated that intentional recording may feel like another responsibility. This is especially important for a product whose intended value is emotional rather than productive.

**Interpretation:** reducing taps is not enough. The product must also reduce the mental burden of deciding what deserves to be recorded and how to describe it.

**Product implication:** avoid making frequency, completeness or visible streaks the main definition of success.

### Signal 02 — Lightweight daily notes may create a reason to return

Small, low-pressure expressions appeared more compatible with everyday relationship behavior than formal memory creation.

**Interpretation:** users may return for a sense of the other person’s presence, while the archive grows as a by-product.

**Product implication:** the Home experience should prioritize emotional immediacy and lightweight interaction instead of functioning only as a dashboard.

### Signal 03 — Recording after completing a shared wish may lower effort

A completed plan already provides context: what happened, why it mattered and who experienced it. Prompting users at that point could make memory creation feel like a continuation of the experience rather than a separate documentation task.

**Interpretation:** the best moment to ask for a record may be when the product already knows part of the story.

**Product implication:** connect Wish Jar completion with an optional path into Memories.

This remains a design direction to validate, not a confirmed behavioral outcome.

### Signal 04 — Automatic or AI-assisted recording may address the real cost

Early insights suggested that automation could help reduce the effort required to preserve shared life.

**Interpretation:** the meaningful opportunity for AI is not generating decorative relationship content. It is helping users organize or draft a record from context they have already created.

**Product implication:** explore assistive capture with user review and control.

AI-assisted recording remains a future product hypothesis unless explicitly implemented and tested.

## 05. Needs Framework

| User need | Current friction | Product opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Feel connected in everyday life | Existing tools prioritize message exchange, not a persistent shared atmosphere | Support lightweight expressions of presence |
| Preserve meaningful moments | Recording requires deliberate effort and content is scattered | Make capture contextual and easier to complete |
| Revisit the relationship story | Memories are stored by file type or chronology rather than shared meaning | Organize content within a relationship space |
| Turn intentions into experiences | Shared ideas disappear in chats or personal notes | Give future wishes a visible shared place |
| Maintain equal ownership | One person may become the “manager” of the relationship archive | Design for reciprocal contribution and transparent shared control |
| Protect intimate content | Relationship data is personal and potentially sensitive | Make privacy, consent and space isolation foundational |

---

## 06. Core User Problem

The research reframed the user problem from:

> “Couples need a better way to record memories.”

to:

> **“Couples already create meaningful traces, but those traces are fragmented, costly to organize and rarely connected into a shared relationship narrative.”**

This distinction changes the role of the product.

LoveMemo should not continuously ask users to produce more content. It should help meaningful traces emerge from shared life, remain understandable later and gradually increase the value of the relationship space.

---

## 07. Design Principles Derived from Research

### Reduce recording pressure

Contribution should feel optional, lightweight and connected to a real moment. The product should not imply that a relationship is healthier because more content has been uploaded.

### Design for presence before archive

A valuable archive may take time to grow. The product needs an immediate reason to return, such as a small sign of the other person’s presence.

### Connect actions across time

Future wishes, present experiences and past memories should not behave like isolated modules. One action should be able to become the context for the next.

### Treat both people as co-owners

The relationship space should not belong operationally or emotionally to only one partner. Invitation, contribution, editing, permissions and exit rules require explicit design in a productized version.

### Use AI to remove work, not manufacture intimacy

Automation should assist with organizing, recalling or drafting from user-provided context. Users must retain control over what is created, edited, shared and preserved.

---

## 08. Working User Model

Rather than defining one fixed persona, LoveMemo uses a relationship-centered model with four recurring moments:

| Moment | User intention | Relevant product area |
|---|---|---|
| Express | “I want to leave something small for us today.” | Home |
| Anticipate | “I want us to remember and do this together.” | Wish Jar |
| Experience | “We are doing something meaningful together.” | Transition between Wish Jar and Memories |
| Revisit | “I want to look back and feel the continuity of our relationship.” | Memories |

This model is intentionally behavioral. The same couple may move between these moments without following a fixed linear journey.

## 09. Key Hypotheses for the Next Stage

The early study produced directions worth testing, not final answers.

### H1. Presence can drive return before the archive becomes valuable

If lightweight shared interaction creates an immediate emotional reward, users may have a reason to revisit the space while memories are still accumulating.

### H2. Contextual prompts can reduce the burden of recording

If LoveMemo prompts users after an existing shared action — such as completing a wish — memory creation may require less effort than starting from a blank entry.

### H3. Accumulated shared history can become a retention asset

As the space gains relationship-specific context, revisiting it may become more valuable over time. This requires longitudinal validation.

### H4. Reciprocity affects perceived value

A shared space may feel rewarding when both people contribute, but burdensome when participation is persistently unequal.

### H5. Privacy and control are part of the emotional experience

Users’ willingness to store intimate content may depend on clear consent, ownership and exit behavior, not only technical security.

---

## 10. What Has Not Yet Been Validated

The current research does **not** establish:

- Market size or broad demand
- Long-term retention after one month or one year
- Whether daily notes consistently increase return behavior
- Whether Wish Jar completion leads to more memory creation
- Whether AI-assisted capture improves contribution without reducing authenticity
- How different participation levels affect relationship-space value
- Willingness to pay
- Production readiness, scalability or security
- Positive effects on relationship quality

These questions belong to future product validation and should not be presented as results of the early study.

---

## 11. Research Conclusion

The strongest early signal was not demand for more relationship features. It was the tension between the desire to preserve shared life and the effort required to do so.

That insight gave LoveMemo a clearer direction:

> **Build a shared space whose value grows from the relationship itself, while asking users to do as little artificial maintenance as possible.**

The next stage should therefore prioritize retention and relationship value over feature expansion. The central question is no longer whether users can create a memory, note or wish in the interface. It is:

> **What makes two people want to return to the same space after one week, one month and one year?**
